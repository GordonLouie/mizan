# Monthly investor update — template

**Send cadence:** First Monday of every month. Same day, every month, without exception. Investors who back you have signed up for the obligation of reading these; you've signed up for the obligation of writing them. Both sides keep the contract.

**Audience:** All signed pre-seed investors + any committed-but-not-yet-closed investors + any past investor leads who explicitly asked to stay in the loop. Keep this list curated. Pruning is your job, not the BCC field's.

**Channel:** A clean email from `jk@mizan.app`. BCC all recipients (never CC). One thread per month — reply-all is welcome, that's how investors help.

**Length:** Two screen-fulls maximum. If you're writing more than that, you're hiding the lede.

## What NOT to put in these

- Hot takes on macroeconomic conditions. Investors have those themselves.
- Vague aspirational statements ("we're building something special"). Either show the metrics or don't.
- Apologetic framing of bad numbers. State them flat; explain in one sentence; move on.
- Politics, religion-as-politics, or anything that draws controversy unrelated to product.
- Internal team drama or HR concerns. If a hire didn't work out, "[X] is no longer with the team" is enough.

## Tracking

Maintain a Notion page with each month's send. At the start of each month, look back at last month's "what I'm working on next" — those become the "what shipped" anchors for this month. Investors notice when commitments are kept; they notice harder when they're not.

## When to break cadence

Twice a year: the annual letter (longer, end of December) and the half-year review (end of June). These have different conventions — more reflective, less metrics-driven, more about the medium-term plan. Plan separately.

## Anti-template: monthly updates that have killed companies

- Five updates in a row that read "we're talking to interesting partners" with no shipped product.
- Sudden drop in metrics with no explanation.
- A surprise piece of bad news (key hire left, scholar quit, lawsuit) in month 6 that didn't appear in months 4-5 even though it was already known.
- Switching the metrics displayed from month to month — pick what you measure on, measure it consistently, only add new metrics.

Investors who back you are betting on you. Honest, regular updates are how you keep that bet alive — including when the news isn't good.
