# Customer interview kit

**Goal of this document.** Equip the founder (and eventually a research-minded hire) to do twenty-five customer interviews in the first ninety days. Twenty-five is the magic number — under it, the patterns aren't reliable; over it, marginal returns drop sharply.

**Cadence.** Two interviews per week, every week, for twelve weeks. Block time on Mondays and Wednesdays; do not move them for anything except critical existential events.

**Tooling.**
- **Booking:** Cal.com (free) with two 30-minute slots per week.
- **Recording:** Otter.ai or Riverside (with explicit consent at the top of every call).
- **Notes:** Notion template (linked below).
- **Compensation:** $25 LaunchGood gift card or US$25 charitable donation in their name, sent within 24 hours. Don't make this conditional on what they say.

---

## Universal opening (60 seconds)

> Hi [name], thanks for taking the time. I'm jk — founder of Mizan. I'm going to ask you some questions about how you think about halal investing and how you currently make decisions. There are no right answers; the goal is just to learn from your real experience.
>
> A few things up front: First, this is a research call, not a sales call — I'm not going to pitch you a subscription. Second, your honest reactions matter much more than polite ones; if Mizan is wrong about something, I'd really rather know. Third, I'd like to record this conversation so I can focus on listening rather than note-taking — is that okay? It stays internal, I won't share it externally.

Wait for confirmation, then go.

## Universal closing (60 seconds)

> Last question — anything I should have asked but didn't?
>
> Then: thank you, here's how I'll follow up.

Send the gift card / donation within 24 hours, plus a one-line email: "Thank you for the time. The [specific thing they said] really helped." Specific. No generic boilerplate.

## Cohort 1: First-time halal investors (target N=8)

**Who:** People who identify as Muslim and are interested in halal investing but have never bought a stock. Found through: masjid networks, Muslim Discord communities, the founder's Instagram followers.

**Question set:**

1. Walk me through the last meaningful financial decision you made — anything from picking a savings account to deciding not to take a job. What was the decision-making process?
2. When you think about investing, what comes to mind first?
3. What's stopped you from starting until now?
4. Do you know which Islamic scholar's rulings you follow most closely? How did you come to follow them?
5. If a friend told you tomorrow that they'd found a great halal-investing app, what's the first thing you'd want to know?
6. Imagine I could give you any product that solves halal-investing for you. What does it look like?
7. If you could only fix ONE thing about how Muslims invest today, what would it be?
8. Where do you currently get answers to halal-finance questions? (Imam? YouTube? Friends?)

**What we're trying to learn:** Whether the wedge is real for the non-investing audience; whether the AAOIFI-default frustration is something you have to be already investing to feel; whether content/education is the right top-of-funnel.

---

## Cohort 2: Active halal-app users (target N=8)

**Who:** Currently paying for Zoya, Musaffa, Islamicly, or Wahed. Found through: r/IslamicFinance, comments on competitor YouTube content, friends-of-friends in Muslim-tech communities.

**Question set:**

1. Which halal-investing app do you currently use? Why that one?
2. How often do you actually use it? What for, specifically?
3. Show me, if you'd like — what's the last thing you checked in the app? [Screenshare]
4. Has any verdict in the app ever surprised you? When?
5. What scholar's rulings do you most closely follow? Does your current app reflect them?
6. Have you ever doubted the verdict and looked elsewhere to verify? Where?
7. If your app added six different scholar profiles instead of just one default, would you use the variety? Why or why not?
8. What would have to be true for you to switch from your current app to a new one?

**What we're trying to learn:** Real switching cost; whether the multi-scholar feature alone is enough to switch; what the failure modes of existing apps are.

---

## Cohort 3: Muslims who don't invest (target N=4)

**Who:** Muslims who explicitly avoid the stock market because of halal concerns. The largest underserved audience — and the one no halal-investing app has activated.

**Question set:**

1. When did you first conclude that the stock market was problematic for you?
2. What got you to that conclusion — a specific scholar, a piece of writing, a conversation?
3. What do you do with your savings instead?
4. If a respected scholar told you tomorrow that there's a defensible Islamic way to invest, what would your reaction be?
5. What would it take for you to try?
6. Is there a number you're saving FOR that the right product could help you reach?

**What we're trying to learn:** Whether the non-investing-Muslim audience is reachable at all; what the conversion narrative looks like; whether trust in named scholars on a supervisory board moves the needle.

---

## Cohort 4: B2B prospects (target N=3)

**Who:** Product or compliance people at brokerages, neobanks, robo-advisors, or 401(k) providers who might be a B2B customer for the Compliance API (year 2). Found through: warm intros, LinkedIn, Twitter-DM founders.

**Question set:**

1. Walk me through how your company currently handles religion-based product accommodation, if at all.
2. Have you ever explored adding a "halal mode" or Shariah-screened option? What stopped you (or got you there)?
3. If a third-party API could give you compliance verdicts under multiple Shariah methodologies with named scholar supervision, would you embed it? What would your concerns be?
4. What would you need to see — credentials, customers, SOC 2 — before approving such a vendor?
5. Where in your product roadmap would this realistically sit?
6. Who else at your company would need to be sold on this? (Eng? Compliance? Marketing? CEO?)
7. How would your company price this internally? Is "$0.005/call to a vendor" something you'd push back on?

**What we're trying to learn:** Whether the B2B API is a real year-2 product or wishful thinking; what the actual sales motion looks like; pricing reality.

---

## Cohort 5: Scholars (target N=2)

**Who:** Scholars whose names might end up on Mizan's Shariah Supervisory Board — or scholars currently on competitors' boards. Found through: direct outreach, conference panels, advisor of an advisor.

**Question set:**

1. How did you come to advise halal-finance products? What drew you to the space?
2. What do you find most frustrating about current halal-finance products?
3. What would it take to make you feel proud of being publicly associated with one?
4. If you imagine the ideal halal-investing platform — not necessarily ours — what does it look like?
5. How do you feel about AI being used in scholarly Q&A? What scope feels acceptable to you?
6. What questions are halal investors actually asking you that current apps don't surface?
7. What's the question a halal-investing product should be asked publicly that nobody asks?

**What we're trying to learn:** What scholars need to feel respected; how to position the AI Q&A scope; whether there's a scholarly product gap we're not seeing.

## How to write up an interview (template)

Save to Notion: `Research / Interviews / [Date] - [Name] - [Cohort]`

```
- Date:
- Cohort:
- Recording link:
- Compensated: Y/N + how
-
- THREE THINGS I'M TAKING AWAY:
  1. [Specific quote or behavior, not opinion]
  2.
  3.
-
- DIRECT QUOTES (verbatim, 3-5):
  - "..."
  - "..."
-
- THINGS I WAS WRONG ABOUT GOING IN:
  -
-
- NEW QUESTIONS THIS RAISED:
  -
-
- ACTION ITEMS:
  -
```

Write the writeup within 24 hours of the interview. Wait longer and the quotes blur.

---

## Synthesis cadence

Every 5 interviews, do a 2-hour synthesis session:

1. Read through all 5 writeups in one sitting.
2. List the top 3 quotes that landed hardest.
3. List the top 3 surprises (things you didn't expect).
4. List the top 3 things you've now learned are wrong about your priors.
5. Decide on 1-2 changes to the product, deck, or messaging.

Make the changes. Continue.

---

## What success looks like at N=25

- The deck has been edited based on 3 specific things you learned.
- The wedge cohort has been refined or confirmed with direct evidence.
- The pricing has been validated (or revised) based on 25 reactions.
- The "first scream" — the headline pain point users articulate without prompting — is now a quote you've heard repeatedly.
- The founder can recite a verbatim quote from 5 specific people that captures their experience.

If you don't have these by interview 25, you didn't listen — you talked. Start again.
