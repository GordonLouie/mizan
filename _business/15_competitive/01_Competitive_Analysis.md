# Competitive analysis — Mizan vs the halal investing landscape

**Date:** May 2026
**Author:** jk + Mizan team
**Purpose:** Inform product, design, and growth decisions with rigorous understanding of what competitors do well and where Mizan can win.

---

## Tier 1 — Direct competitors (consumer halal stock screening)

### Zoya  ·  zoya.finance

**What they do.** Mobile-first halal stock screener for individual investors. AAOIFI-based methodology. Available iOS + Android + web. ~100K downloads, est. 15-25K paying.

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- Verdict language: "Halal / Doubtful / Not Halal" — single-glance, familiar, plain-Arabic-English. We currently use Pass/Borderline/Fail — switch immediately.
- 3-item primary navigation (Stocks · Portfolio · Account · More). Discipline in what shows up top.
- Mobile-first UX. Touch targets sized properly, modals scale, gestures feel native.
- Single primary action on landing: search any stock.
- Onboarding kept to 3 steps maximum.
- "Why?" expansion sits inside each verdict, not on a separate page.
- Pricing: 5 free screens/day + $9.99/mo unlimited. Industry-standard SaaS model.

**Their gaps — where we win.**
- Single scholarly methodology (AAOIFI only). No multi-scholar option. **Our biggest advantage.**
- No transparent ratio math shown to user (black-box compliance score).
- No capital-gains purification automation.
- Educational content thin and embedded in product, not standalone.
- No "halal alternatives" when a stock fails the screen.
- Limited scholarly governance visibility — board not named publicly.

**Net read.** Zoya is the user-experience benchmark we have to match. Multi-scholar architecture + transparent math + editorial education = our differentiation.

---

### Musaffa  ·  musaffa.com

**What they do.** Web-first halal stock screener with deeper research focus. 30,000+ stock ratings. Premium ~$9.99/month. Names Shariah Advisory Board publicly with photos and credentials.

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- Public Shariah Supervisory Board with names + bios + photos. **High trust signal.** When we sign our scholars, this is exactly the treatment they should get.
- Per-stock deep financial reports with historical ratio trends — visibility into how a verdict could change.
- 30,000+ stock coverage — breadth signals legitimacy.
- Educational content built into each compliance card.

**Their gaps — where we win.**
- Single scholarly methodology.
- Feels like Bloomberg-for-Muslims — research-heavy, less consumer-friendly.
- Heavy on the data side, light on the brand side — no clear narrative voice.
- No mobile app polish to match Zoya.

**Net read.** Musaffa is the research-credibility benchmark. We mimic their scholarly board treatment and depth of per-stock data; we beat them on consumer UX and methodology variety.

---

### Islamicly

**What they do.** Older halal screener, India-focused, 30,000+ international stocks. Less polished than Zoya/Musaffa.

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- International stock coverage (UK, India, KSA, UAE listings) — Western competitors mostly US-only.

**Their gaps — where we win.**
- Older design language, less modern UX.
- Single methodology.
- Weaker brand.

**Net read.** Don't compete on stock count alone; compete on methodology depth + education. International expansion is a year-2 priority anyway.

---

### HalalScreener  /  Hudood

**What they do.** Smaller, emerging multi-methodology screeners. HalalScreener has an AI scholar Q&A across 5 madhabs. Hudood cross-checks compliance across multiple standards.

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- Multi-standard architecture — the closest competitors to our thesis.

**Their gaps — where we win.**
- Less polished UX.
- No named scholarly governance.
- Smaller audiences, weaker brand.
- Methodology depth is shallower (toggles between standards rather than deep per-scholar implementation).

**Net read.** They've validated the demand for our wedge. We win on execution, depth, scholarly relationships, and brand.

## Tier 2 — Tangential competitors (managed halal money)

### Wahed Invest  ·  wahed.com

**What they do.** Halal robo-advisor managing money on behalf of clients. Available US, UK, Malaysia, MENA. AAOIFI-based portfolios. 0.99% AUM annual fee. Backed by major MENA capital. Most-funded halal fintech in the world.

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- Trust signaling everywhere: scholars named publicly, audit reports linked from main nav, regulatory licenses listed in the footer of every page.
- Multi-asset framing: stocks + sukuk + gold. Even though we're not asset management, we can mention sukuk + gold alternatives in the AI Q&A.
- Goal-based framing ("Invest for your future") — emotionally resonant.
- Localized versions for different markets — US site differs from Malaysia site in tone, examples, and currency.
- Strong content marketing — podcast, blog, founder presence.

**Their gaps — where we win.**
- Wahed manages money; we're a research tool. Different layer of the stack — we're complementary, not competing.
- Single methodology (AAOIFI).
- Wahed customers still need a screener for their non-Wahed holdings (their employer 401(k), their crypto, their gifts of stock) — that's our opportunity.

**Net read.** Don't compete head-on. Position as the layer above Wahed for self-directed investors and the layer below them for screening verification.

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- 1980s pedigree — pure trust signal. The "we've been here for decades" framing.
- Available inside 401(k) brokerage windows — institutional distribution.
- Annual Shariah audit reports published publicly.

**Their gaps — where we win.**

- No screener product — purely fund management.
- Not active in social / community.

**Net read.** Not a real consumer competitor. Their institutional credibility is something we can't fake but can complement (we don't compete on funds; we screen).

## Tier 3 — Content / community plays

### IslamicFinanceGuru (IFG)  ·  islamicfinanceguru.com

**What they do.** Content-first platform. Newsletter, blog, podcast. Halal alternatives discovery (mortgages, savings, pensions, investments). Recommends specific products (referral model).

**What they do well — what to steal.**
- Newsletter as a primary acquisition channel.
- Halal alternatives directory — they tell you not just what's haram, but what's the halal alternative. We should do this for stocks (when a stock fails, recommend similar passing stocks).
- Founder presence — Ibrahim and Mohsin are recognizable names.
- Community engagement via comments + their Slack.

**Their gaps — where we win.**
- Pure content; no product. We have a product they don't.
- Their "screener" is the editor's pick — not algorithmic, not personalized to a user's chosen scholar.

**Net read.** IFG is a partner, not a competitor. We can syndicate our blog posts to them, get featured, build referral traffic.

## Mizan's unique edge — the five wedges

These are things NO competitor does. We must lead with them in product, marketing, and pitch:

1. **Multi-scholar architecture as default.** Not a toggle. The whole engine is built around it.
2. **Mufti Taqi Usmani's illiquid-asset floor** implemented in the screen. No major app does this.
3. **Capital-gains purification automation** alongside dividend purification. Most apps miss the gains portion entirely.
4. **Transparent ratio math** — we show the numbers behind every verdict. Competitors return a single score.
5. **Editorial education layer** (`/learn`, the glossary, the blog) — written for newcomers, not for finance majors.

---

## What we should add that no one has — the standout features

Beyond matching the field, here's what would put Mizan ahead:

1. **Universal "all 6 scholars at once" view.** Every search shows the verdict under each major methodology side-by-side. The asymmetry IS the product. Currently we show this only for AMZN/META/NFLX as a hardcoded demo — universalize it.

2. **Halal alternatives suggestion.** When a stock fails screening, suggest 3 similar passing stocks in the same sector. ("AMZN failed Mufti Taqi → try WMT, COST, NKE which pass.") IFG does this for products; nobody does it for stocks.

3. **Methodology changelog.** Public timeline of when methodologies change and how that affected which stocks pass. Builds trust like a financial firm's audit history.

4. **Watchlist with verdict-change alerts.** Users mark stocks they own; we email them if a quarterly filing makes a verdict change.

5. **"What if?" sandbox.** Adjust thresholds on a slider; see how the screen changes in real-time. The visual proof that scholarly variation matters.

6. **Scholar Q&A with citations always inline.** Anthropic Claude with the system prompt published openly (radical transparency). Compare to competitors' opaque AI features.

7. **Editorial newsletter (monthly).** One essay per month on a current halal-finance question. Build the brand voice that IFG has but better-designed.

- B2B compliance API private beta.

## What we should NOT do

- Compete with Wahed on managed money. Different layer of the stack.
- Mirror Musaffa's research depth before we have the data. Quality > quantity in coverage.
- Add Zoya's exact features one-for-one. Steal the language and structure; differentiate on the methodology + transparency angle.
- Promise things our scholarly board hasn't approved yet.

> Mizan is the halal investing platform that respects scholarly diversity instead of flattening it.

This is the wedge. The work is to make every product surface, every line of marketing copy, and every conversation reinforce that.
