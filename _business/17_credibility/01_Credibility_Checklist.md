# The credibility checklist — what Mizan needs to be trusted

Mizan touches something Muslims take seriously: their relationship with riba, with halal earnings, with what they own. Without credibility, no amount of slick UX matters — people won't use a tool they don't trust on a question this personal.

This is the work that turns a working product into a trusted institution.

Items are ranked by how much each one moves the trust needle.

---

## Tier 0  ·  The non-negotiables (do these before you share with anyone)

These four cannot be skipped. If any is missing, every other thing on this list is undermined.

- [ ] **Name a real Shariah Supervisory Board chair.** Until you can put a recognized scholar's name on the homepage, Mizan is "a tech tool" — not "a Shariah-supervised platform." The single highest-leverage action available to you. The drafted email to Mufti Faraz Adam is in `_business/04_growth/02_Scholar_Outreach_Emails.md`. Send it this week. Without a yes from him or someone of equivalent stature, distribution is premature.

- [ ] **Publish the Shariah board page publicly on mizan.app/about** with name, photo, credentials, institutional affiliation, and your written terms of engagement. Musaffa and Wahed both do this. It's the first thing a sceptical Muslim user looks for.

- [ ] **Publish your methodology with primary-source citations.** `/methodology` exists. Update it so every threshold cites the AAOIFI Standard No. 21 (page number), Mufti Taqi Usmani's "Introduction to Islamic Finance" (page number), or equivalent. No claims without sources. Sceptics check.

- [ ] **Add an honest "as of" date and data source on every verdict.** Already partially done in the new compliance card. Make it consistent — every verdict says "Computed [date] from [source]." This signals professional discipline.

## Tier 1  ·  Founder credibility (do this in the first 30 days)

The founder IS the brand at this stage. Mizan has no track record on its own; you have to lend it yours.

- [ ] **Publish a real "About me" page or section.** Photo, real name (or the founder identity you're comfortable with publicly), brief story of why you're building Mizan. Mufti Faraz Adam, Mohsin from IFG — every Muslim-finance founder is identifiable. You should be too.

- [ ] **Set up a credible founder presence on X / Twitter.** Avatar with your face. Bio: "Building @mizanapp · halal investing tooling · HK." Pin a tweet that introduces Mizan with the AMZN demo. Post 3-4 times a week minimum.

- [ ] **Write a LinkedIn post announcing Mizan publicly.** Once.  Brief story. Link. Don't oversell — let the product speak.

- [ ] **Record one founder-on-camera video for the YouTube channel.** Even 3 minutes. Show your face. Explain why you built this. People trust faces.

- [ ] **Make the email replies actually personal.** Sign as "jk" not "Mizan Team." Users notice.

---

## Tier 2  ·  Methodological transparency (do in parallel with Tier 1)

Trust in finance is earned through openness. Black boxes lose.

- [ ] **Methodology page should cite the source for every threshold.** AAOIFI Standard No. 21 (link to AAOIFI website). Mufti Taqi Usmani's published work (cite chapter + page). Ayatollah Sistani's office rulings (link to their site). DJIM (S&P methodology PDF link).

- [ ] **Publish a methodology changelog.** A `/methodology/changelog` page showing every change to a scholar profile, with date, scholar approval signature, and rationale. Even if it's currently empty, the page itself signals discipline.

- [ ] **Add per-stock data provenance.** Each compliance card should say which 10-Q or 10-K the data came from. Until live data is wired, say "approximation based on most recent filing as of [date]."

- [ ] **Publish your scholarly disagreements honestly.** If two of your board members disagree on a point, the methodology page should say so. Pretending the scholars agree on everything is the fastest way to lose credibility with anyone who knows the field.

- [ ] **Open the AI scholar Q&A system prompt publicly.** Yes, really. Put it at `/methodology/ai-prompt`. The fact that you can show your prompt is the proof that you've designed it carefully. Other apps hide theirs because theirs are embarrassing.

## Tier 3  ·  Community and endorsements

Trust compounds through other trusted voices.

- [ ] **Get one named scholar quote.** Even before they formally join the board. "[Scholar name] reviewed Mizan's methodology and finds it consistent with [his/her] published rulings." One sentence on the homepage. Massive trust signal.

- [ ] **Get one masjid to mention you.** Imam announces Mizan once on a Friday or in a newsletter. Counts as endorsement. Approach 5 masjids; get one yes; that's the testimonial.

- [ ] **Pitch IFG (IslamicFinanceGuru) to write about Mizan.** They review halal-finance tools regularly. A neutral or positive review there is the warm-intro to ~30K Muslim investors. Pitch is in `_business/04_growth/05_Podcast_Pitches.md`.

- [ ] **Pitch the Muslim Money Show podcast.** A 60-minute conversation reaches their audience and goes evergreen.

- [ ] **Get one Twitter quote from a recognized Muslim finance voice.** Mufti Faraz Adam, Joe Bradford, Shabir Ally, anyone who already has credibility. Even 5 words.

- [ ] **Build a Discord community.** First 100 invitees from your network. Give it a name like "Mizan readers." Become the watering hole for thoughtful halal-finance conversation. Costs nothing.

## Tier 4  ·  Operational and legal (do once you have first 100 users)

These items signal "real company" — important for both users and investors.

- [ ] **Incorporate the operating entity.** Cayman holdco + HK Operating per the playbook. Once incorporated, list your registered company info in the footer. Anonymous web apps lose trust fast for anything finance-adjacent.

- [ ] **Apply to Cyberport.** When you're accepted, the "Cyberport Incubation" badge is a third-party signal of legitimacy (even if non-dilutive). Pre-filled application in `_business/05_applications/01_Cyberport_Application.md`.

- [ ] **Apply to other Islamic-finance accelerators.** HKSTP, INSEAD Islamic Banking & Finance program network. Doesn't matter if you get in — the application alone clarifies your story.

- [ ] **Engage Deacons HK for the 1-hour SFC structuring call.** When asked "are you registered with any regulator?" you should have an answer — even if the answer is "we're an informational service, confirmed with counsel; not required to register." Get that confirmation in writing from a real law firm.

- [ ] **Publish a Trust Center page.** `/trust` (or expand `/methodology`). One page with: incorporation status, scholarly board, methodology sources, data sources, privacy posture, security posture, contact for concerns. Wahed has this; we should too.

- [ ] **Get press@mizan.app monitored daily.** Reply to every email within 24 hours. Slow responses kill credibility fast.

## Tier 5  ·  Ongoing trust-building (forever)

These aren't one-time items. They're disciplines.

- [ ] **Publish a monthly product update post.** What shipped, what users are saying, what changed in any methodology. Compound over a year you have a credibility archive.

- [ ] **Send a monthly investor update** (even pre-funding). Template in `_business/09_investor_relations/01_Monthly_Update_Template.md`. Same content forms the basis for the public update above.

- [ ] **Annual public Shariah Report.** Co-signed by every board member. Lists every methodology change in the year, every audit issue raised, every resolution. The single biggest annual trust signal in the field.

- [ ] **Respond to every methodology challenge personally and publicly.** When a user emails saying "your implementation of Mufti Taqi's rule is wrong," respond on the public methodology page. Either acknowledge and fix, or explain why your reading is consistent. Either way, transparency wins.

- [ ] **Publish your sources.** Every blog post, every methodology entry, every fact should be sourced. Footnotes are a discipline; build them in.

- [ ] **Run a quarterly methodology audit** with your Shariah board. Even if nothing changes, the act of meeting + reviewing is the work. Publish the minutes.

## The single most important question to answer publicly

For every page someone might land on, they should be able to find the answer to this question in under 60 seconds:

> **"Who decided what's halal here, and why should I believe them?"**

If your /methodology page, /about page, and homepage don't make this answerable, no amount of UX polish matters. If they do, you're ready.
