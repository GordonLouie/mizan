# Call with Prof. James Frankel — Prep (plain-English)

## Logistics
- **When:** Saturday 8pm New York = **Sunday 8am Hong Kong**.
- **Where:** your Zoom link (already sent). Send the calendar invite if you haven't.
- **Who he is:** James D. Frankel, Ph.D. — Director, Centre for the Study of Islamic Culture; Associate Professor at CUHK. He's an **academic expert on Islam** (he studies and teaches it) — but probably **not** the kind of religious authority who officially issues rulings on money. That difference matters: see "What to ask him for."

## The one goal
This is **not** a sales pitch. The goal is to **start a relationship and get his honest opinion** on how to make HalalGauge trustworthy. Trust/credibility is your biggest missing piece, and he's the most senior person you can reach who can help with it. A good outcome = he agrees to stay involved somehow, or points you to the right people.

## Your 2-minute intro (say roughly this)
> "I'm a CUHK med student, but I've built HalalGauge — an app that checks whether a stock is halal. What I care about is honesty: instead of one black-box 'halal / not halal' score, it lets you pick which set of religious rules you follow and then shows the exact numbers and the published source behind every answer. It's live, covers about 650 stocks, and it's free. What I *don't* have is religious backing — and that's exactly why I wanted your opinion."

Then stop and let him react. Have the live site open to screen-share one stock (e.g. AMZN) showing the numbers + the side-by-side comparison.

## What to ask him (let him be the expert)
1. **How do we earn trust:** "What would it actually take for something like this to be trusted by scholars and the community — a named advisor, a review panel, an official certificate? What's realistic for one person?"
2. **Biggest risk:** "We show five different sets of rules side by side. Where's the biggest risk in presenting it that way?"
3. **The 'which group of Islam' question:** "People ask 'which option matches my background?' But the five options are *modern screening rule-sets*, not the traditional branches of Islam — they don't line up one-to-one. How would you help people choose without confusing the two?"
4. **The Shia option:** "For the Shia option we apply *khums* — a 20% yearly religious payment on leftover savings. Is the way we've simplified that reasonable?"
5. **The 'strictest' option:** "We offer a zero-tolerance option that fails almost every stock. Is offering that responsible, or is it misleading?"
6. **Who gives the final religious sign-off:** "For the actual religious approval of the finance rules, who should I be talking to — and could you introduce me?"

## What to ask him *for* (decide based on how it goes)
- **Most realistic:** ask him to be an **informal advisor**, and to **introduce you** to a scholar who specialises in Islamic finance. Don't ask him to personally "approve" the religious rules on the spot — that's likely outside what he does, and asking puts him on the spot.
- **Softer version:** just ask permission to **send him the one-page brief** and **keep in touch** as you sort out proper religious oversight.

## How to come across — do / don't
- **Do** show up as a student asking for guidance. Humility is your best card with a professor.
- **Do** stress the honesty angle (you show the sources and the math, no black box). That's what earns academic respect.
- **Don't** oversell: no scholar panel yet, no revenue, still in early testing. Say it plainly — he'll trust you more.
- **Don't** push for an endorsement or a deal. This call is about learning and relationship, not closing.
- **Do** take notes and finish by suggesting one clear next step.

### Quick word cheat-sheet (so nothing trips you up)
- **Halal** — allowed under Islamic rules. (Opposite: *haram* = not allowed.)
- **Riba** — interest; money made just from lending money. Not allowed in Islam — it's the main thing stock-screening checks for.
- **Purification** — giving the small "not-clean" slice of your investment income (e.g. a company's bit of interest income) to charity, so the rest is clean.
- **AAOIFI** — a standards body that writes the common rulebook Islamic banks follow. Your default option.
- **Khums** — in Shia practice, a 20% yearly payment on leftover savings/surplus.
- **Fiqh** — Islamic law: the worked-out religious rules. (If he says "fiqh," he means "the religious rulebook.")
- **Madhhab** — a "school" or tradition within Islam. Sunni ones: Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali. Shia main one: Ja'fari. These are *not* the same as your five screening options — that's the point of question #3.
- **Mufti / faqih** — a scholar officially qualified to give religious rulings. (A *fatwa* = a ruling.) This is the kind of person you eventually need for sign-off — and what you'd ask Frankel to introduce you to.
